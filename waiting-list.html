How we read your application

What we value most

Self-help group, youth club, farmers’ group, temple, mosque or church committee, school parents’ group, relief work: all of it counts as public work. Roughly in this order:

  1. 1What you have actually doneOne real example beats a long list of titles.
  2. 2Showing up, again and againMonths and years of steady work, not one big event.
  3. 3Knowing a local issue wellWhat you have seen first-hand, who it hurts, what you tried.
  4. 4HonestyIncluding what did not work. Say what someone could confirm.
  5. 5Bringing people togetherWho you worked with, who you helped, who helped you.
  6. 6Willingness to learnTaking feedback, and trying again after a setback.
  7. 7Being able to take partTell us what would help, so we can plan for it.

You do not need: a degree, fluent English, a political family or title, a polished resume, or long answers. Write in Tamil or English.

This is guidance, not a scoring model. There are no points, and a strong application does not guarantee a place.

Fictional examples

What a simple, honest one looks like

Meena Kumar

Ward 14, Sulur, Coimbatore · 98765 00000 · meena.example@email.com

About me

I lead a women’s self-help group in my ward. For six years I have helped neighbours get water, ration and pension problems fixed. I want to learn how local government really works so I can get more things fixed, for more people.

What I have done
  • Lead a 20-member women’s self-help group (2019 to now): weekly meetings, savings and loan records. Trained two members to keep the books.
  • Got the broken borewell in our street repaired (2023): collected signatures from the street, went to the ward office four times, and followed up until it was fixed.
  • Helped run the yearly health camp with the primary health centre. Organised 15 volunteers on the day.
  • Helped families fix ration card and pension forms.
Work
  • Tailoring unit owner, Sulur (2015 to now)
  • Cashier, village cooperative store (2012 to 2015)
Education and training
  • Class 12, Government Girls Higher Secondary School, Sulur (2011)
  • Bookkeeping training for self-help groups, local NGO (2020)
Languages

Tamil (native) · English (basic, improving) · Malayalam (spoken)

People who can confirm my work

The health-centre nurse and a self-help group federation officer. Names and numbers on request.

  • Work first. The “What I have done” part comes before jobs and school.
  • Plain and specific. What she did, how long, what changed.
  • Someone can confirm it. Honest about what she can back up.

Ward 14, Sulur, Coimbatore · 15 September

Dear Selection Team,

My name is Meena Kumar. I run a tailoring unit and lead a 20-member women’s self-help group in Ward 14, Sulur.

Two years ago the borewell in our street stopped working. Sixty families were fetching water from far away. I collected signatures from the street, went to the ward office four times and kept going back until it was repaired. It took six weeks. Nobody taught me how to do this. I learned by trying, and I also learned how much I do not know about budgets, rules and who is responsible for what.

The issue I understand best is drinking water and ration shop service in my ward. I have seen it every week for six years.

I am applying now because my group keeps asking me for help beyond what I know. I want to learn properly so that I can fix more problems, and teach others to do the same.

If I am selected, I will attend every session and finish what I start. I can travel to Coimbatore. I would like classes in Tamil.

Thank you for reading this.
Yours sincerely,
Meena Kumar

  • One real story instead of general praise.
  • Says what she does not know. Honesty about gaps is a strength.
  • Short. Under 250 words. Tamil is welcome too.
